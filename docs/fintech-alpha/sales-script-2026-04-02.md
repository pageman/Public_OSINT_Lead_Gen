# Fintech Alpha Sales Script

Date: April 2, 2026

## Use Case

Use this for:

- founder-led sales calls
- intro emails after someone reads the fintech page
- briefing calls after an insights or pilot-intake submission

## One-Line Opening

We track the public operational signals most likely to create cross-border payments pain before they show up as obvious outages, then we help teams execute on the workflow bottlenecks those signals expose.

## The Problem Framing

Most payments teams already see the news. What they do not have is a clean operational read on:

- which policy changes are likely to increase manual review load
- which payout corridors or partners are becoming brittle
- where compliance, fraud, and exception handling are colliding into one queue

That is where the actual operating pain starts.

## The April 2, 2026 Alpha

Right now the strongest public signals are:

1. Regulatory and policy change density is elevated.
   The Federal Reserve issued a March 18, 2026 statement and implementation note, and recent enforcement and policy activity is still elevated.

2. Public platform status is not enough.
   A provider can show “All Systems Operational” while still carrying recent incidents that create corridor-specific support, reconciliation, or retry pressure.

3. Compliance and exception queue collision risk is rising.
   The operational burden is increasingly in queue design, prioritization, and escalation logic, not just in raw event count.

## Why That Matters

If your team waits for a visible outage, you are already late. The expensive part usually lands first in:

- manual review backlog
- reconciliation rework
- partner escalation load
- support spillover from repeated retries or delays

## What We Give Away For Free

We publish a public operations pulse that answers:

- what changed this week
- what stayed stable
- what operators should watch next

The goal is to make the signal clear enough that an ops lead can use it immediately.

## What We Sell

When that signal exposes a real workflow bottleneck, we offer a 30-day operations acceleration sprint focused on:

- queue triage
- corridor escalation mapping
- manual-review prioritization rules
- reconciliation and exception handling support
- a surge playbook for the highest-friction workflow

## Qualification Questions

Use these on calls:

1. Which corridor, partner, or queue is creating the most avoidable manual work right now?
2. Is the pain showing up as backlog, failed payouts, support volume, reconciliation effort, or compliance review load?
3. Do you need a fix this week, this month, or are you still mapping the problem?
4. Is the issue broad platform reliability, or is it concentrated in a small number of brittle partner or corridor combinations?

## Transition To Pilot

If the buyer confirms real workflow pain, say:

The next step is not a long discovery project. We run a short operations acceleration sprint around one high-friction queue so you can reduce rework, tighten prioritization, and get a cleaner escalation model quickly.

## Close

If it would help, we can take one queue or one corridor that is currently creating the most friction and turn the public signal into a concrete triage and execution plan.

## Objection Handling

### “We already monitor status pages and regulatory updates.”

That is useful, but it usually does not tell you which queue will break first or where your team is over-spending manual work.

### “We do not have a visible outage.”

That is usually the point. The hidden cost often arrives first in review load, retries, reconciliation effort, and support operations.

### “We are not ready for a platform purchase.”

This is not a platform-first sale. The first step is a focused sprint around one workflow bottleneck.
